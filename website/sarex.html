Incident Command Post assembled at base; aircrews and ground teams participate from home or remote stations.

  • ✓ Ideal For: Group-level and Wing-level training incorporating distant squadrons into a unified incident.

Zero physical travel required. Every crew member connects over secure WireGuard VPN or Caddy HTTPS.

  • ✓ Ideal For: Monthly weeknight tactical drills, bad-weather contingencies, and specialized radio staff training.
Command & Control Architecture

Simulating the Incident Command System (ICS) Virtually

CAP V-SAR is not an isolated flight simulator—it is a full-theater command exercise. Every participant occupies a defined FEMA ICS seat with authentic tools, communications channels, and operational responsibilities.

🎖️

Command & Air Ops

STRATEGIC & TACTICAL OVERSIGHT
Incident Commander (IC):

Establishes incident objectives, authorizes search areas, oversees safety, and coordinates with the Air Force Rescue Coordination Center (AFRCC) and County Sheriff.

Air Operations Branch Director (AOBD):

Assigns search grids, manages loiter fuel endurance, deconflicts multiple aircraft altitudes (e.g. 5,500 ft vs 6,500 ft MSL), and reviews automated telemetry debriefs.

Planning & Operations Chiefs:

Calculate Probability of Detection (POD), build Incident Action Plans (IAPs), and dispatch ground assets based on airborne bearing vectors.

📻

Communications Unit

NET CONTROL & FORM 110 LOGGING
Mission Radio Operator 1 (MRO 1):

Primary tactical net controller. Operates the Web Base Radio console (/radio) on CAP tactical VHF-FM channels. Enforces radiotelephone doctrine (CAPR 100-3).

Mission Radio Operator 2 (MRO 2):

Airband watch controller. Monitors VHF-AM aeronautical search frequencies (123.100 MHz SAR, 122.900 MHz flight line) and logs pilot transit check-ins.

CAP Form 110 Continuous Logging:

Every station check-in, OPS NORMAL report, bearing transmission, and altitude change is recorded in real time with UTC timestamps for formal post-mission audit.

✈️

Field Search Assets

HOMING CREWS & GROUND TEAMS
SAR Mission Pilots (MP):

Fly the C182T Skylane through search patterns, execute steep-turn heading corrections called by the MO, manage engine cooling, and maintain ATC separation.

Mission Observers (MO):

Operate the Becker 517 / RT-600 DF heads and TDFM-136B tactical radio. Track 406 burst cadences, verify station passage over beacons, and call coordinates.

Urban DF (UDF) Ground Teams:

Mobile vehicle and foot search teams connected via smartphone Web PTT. Receive airborne bearing lines and conduct final ground signal homing.

Common Operating Picture (COP)

Deep CalTopo Integration

CalTopo is the gold-standard mapping tool used across Civil Air Patrol, county search-and-rescue teams, and state emergency services. CAP V-SAR integrates directly with CalTopo to maintain a unified, real-time Common Operating Picture.

  • ✓ Live Telemetry Streaming: Aircraft GPS fixes, altitude MSL, and true headings stream continuously into CalTopo map layers, giving the Incident Commander total situational awareness.
  • ✓ Line of Position (LOP) Triangulation: When an airborne observer or ground team logs a bearing, a visual LOP vector is instantly projected across CalTopo topography, highlighting cross-bearing intersection ellipses.
  • ✓ Bi-Directional Search Patterns: Creeping Line, Expanding Square, and Sector search grids planned in the N499CP Flight Planner export seamlessly as GPX/KML for CalTopo and FMS flight plans for the cockpit G1000.
  • ✓ Inter-Agency Interoperability: Incident Commanders can share the live CalTopo exercise URL with County Sheriff SAR coordinators, Cal OES liaisons, and ground teams for authentic multi-agency collaboration.
Operational Scenarios

Tested SAR Operational Scenarios

CAP V-SAR comes pre-loaded with comprehensive exercise scenarios designed to test aircrews, radio operators, and command staff under authentic stress conditions.

Scenario Alpha 406.025 MHz / 121.5 MHz

1. Pauma Valley Mountain ELT Search

A non-flight-plan Cessna 172 fails to arrive at Borrego Valley. AFRCC reports a single COSPAS-SARSAT 406 composite alert in rugged terrain east of Palomar Mountain.

  • ✓ Radio Horizon Climb: Aircraft must climb past 4,500 ft MSL to break line-of-sight terrain shielding from Ramona Mission Base.
  • ✓ 406 Burst Tracking: Becker 517 captures 50-second digital bursts, displaying hex ID and relative bearing; crew tracks analog 121.5 chirp between bursts.
  • ✓ Station Passage: Observer verifies station passage via antenna cone-of-confusion needle swing, logs coordinates, and directs ground UDF team.
Scenario Bravo VHF-FM Tactical Repeater

2. California Wing Mountain Repeater Net

Multi-station wide-area coordination across San Diego and Riverside counties utilizing authentic California Wing high-elevation repeaters.

  • ✓ Tactical Repeater Selection: Aircrews select Mt. Laguna (6,000 ft MSL) or Otay Mountain repeaters with exact CTCSS subaudible access tones.
  • ✓ Shadowing & Hand-offs: As aircraft descend into canyon valleys, repeater coverage drops, forcing crews to switch to simplex AIR 1 or request airborne relay.
  • ✓ MRO Net Discipline: Base MRO manages high-density radio traffic, enforcing strict prowords and CAP Form 110 logging.
Scenario Charlie Air-to-Ground Coordination

3. Tactical Air-to-Ground SAR Coordination

Airborne crew isolates beacon to a 1-square-mile grid sector and guides a mobile ground Urban Direction Finding (UDF) vehicle team in for physical recovery.

  • ✓ Cross-Band Net Bridge: Aircrew communicates on CAP VHF-FM tactical while base relays instructions to ground units on CALCORD inter-agency frequencies.
  • ✓ Visual Air-to-Ground Signals: Pilot performs high-visibility racetrack pattern directly over beacon wreckage to provide visual reference to ground team.
  • ✓ Ground Triangulation: Ground team uses mobile smartphone PTT to confirm beacon signal reception and complete recovery.
Scenario Delta Contingency Drill

4. Inadvertent Stuck-MIC Net Recovery

An observer microphone gets wedged between cockpit seats, keying the primary VHF-FM net and threatening to jam critical mission communications.

  • ✓ Hardware TOT Intervention: TDFM-136B internal 60-second Time-Out Timer trips automatically, unkeying the carrier and displaying *** TIME OUT ***.
  • ✓ Independent Guard Alert: Base MRO breaks through on the TDFM independent Guard receiver (GD1 / AIRGUARD) to alert the aircrew of the stuck mic.
  • ✓ Net Re-establishment: Observer switches GMA 1347 audio panel to COM2 auxiliary, clearing the primary mission net for active search traffic.
Zero-Friction Participation

Instant Setup & Scannable QR Badges

Setting up a distributed exercise takes less than 60 seconds. The Exercise Director opens /setup, selects the participating fleet, and generates unique scannable access codes for every seat.

  • ✓ Fleet Sizing Stepper: Configure 1 to 4 airplanes instantly with custom tail numbers (e.g. N499CP, N99885), callsigns, and DF heads.
  • ✓ Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Dedicated access codes for Instructors (INSTR-1), Pilots (PLT-423), Observers (OBS-423), MROs (MRO-1), and UDF Teams (UDF-1).
  • ✓ Phone & Tablet QR Scanning: Participants scan their assigned QR card with a smartphone camera, instantly opening their role console with microphone permissions pre-configured.
  • ✓ Printable Badges: One-click print stylesheet outputs formatted access cards for lanyards and clipboards.
