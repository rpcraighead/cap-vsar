# CAP V-SAR user-experience review and proposal — 2026-10-11

**Status: review and proposal. Four defects found during the walkthrough are
fixed in the same commit series (section 2); everything else is proposed.**

Method: the exercise console and the flight planner were run locally from
`main` (post waves A–D), seeded with a roster, a running sortie, two reporting
aircraft, a bearing and a message, and every page was walked in Chrome at
desktop width. Screenshots are in `docs/evidence/ux-2026-10-11/`. The seat
stories in `CAP_V-SAR_Seat_User_Stories.docx`, the rehearsal narrative in
`USE_CASE_SAREX_REHEARSAL.md`, the identity plan in
`WEB_HOSTING_AND_IDENTITY_PLAN.md` and the CalTopo plan in
`CALTOPO_PHASE2_PLAN.md` were read first so this proposal builds on decisions
already taken rather than re-opening them. The CalTopo integration has its own
document: [`CALTOPO_INTEGRATION_PLAN.md`](CALTOPO_INTEGRATION_PLAN.md).

The one-sentence version: the system has every *capability* the rehearsal
needs, but it is organised as six separate tools with three visual languages,
and nothing in it answers the questions people actually ask during an
exercise: *who is here, is everyone ready, what is each aircraft doing right
now, who is overdue, what happened when, and how did we do compared with last
time.*

on the base console (the training point is made by the UI itself); the

every page is a static file over one API, which makes the proposal below
cheap to build.

## 2. Defects found during the walkthrough (fixed in this series)

| ID | Defect | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| U-1 | **The planner's SAR pattern generator cannot be opened.** `templates/index.html` never closes the "Updating Aviation Database" modal, so the SAR modal is nested inside a `display:none` element and the SAR button does nothing. Present on `main` before the review; confirmed with the DOM (`modal-sar` is a child of `modal-db`, bounding box 0×0). | Close the `modal-db` div. |
| U-2 | **Every QR badge on `/setup` is blank.** The vendored `qrcode.min.js` is a 14 KB hand-rolled "based on QRCode.js" file whose Reed-Solomon block table is truncated (`RS_BLOCK_TABLE[row] \|\| RS_BLOCK_TABLE[0]`); a 67-character join URL throws "code length overflow" at the shipped level and would produce wrong ECC blocks at the others. No participant has ever been able to scan a badge. | Render badges server-side as SVG with `segno` (pure Python, BSD) at `/api/setup/badge/{id}.svg`; drop the client library. |
| U-3 | **Instructor console shows `BRG NaN° rel · nm · sig NaN%`** whenever the DF dictionary is redacted. On an explicitly open dev console with no instructor credential, the anonymous viewer may *act* as the instructor but is still shown redacted state, which is X-1's invariant and is kept. | The aircraft line renders only the fields it has ("receiving · bearing withheld for this seat"). Log in as the instructor, or set `CAPVSAR_TOKEN_INSTRUCTOR` and send it, to see live DF on a dev console. |
| U-4 | The setup hint says "enter the 6-character code"; codes are 40 characters. | Wording now matches; short codes are proposed in §4. |

The join page's bare HTML (no exercise name, seat or callsign) is not a bug
but is the first thing a participant sees after scanning a badge; it is the
first item of the proposal.

## 3. Findings, by the question a participant is asking

### 3.1 "How do I get in, and did it work?" (invitation and onboarding)

- Codes are 32 hex characters behind a role prefix. Nobody types them; the
  only usable path is the copy-link button, pasted into some other channel.
  The badges were meant to be the path and have never rendered (U-2).
- The director has no view of who has accepted, who is connected, or from
  where. "7 Participants" counts rows generated, not people present.
- Roster entries are role slots ("Staff Member 1 / BASE 1", "MRO 2"), not
  seats. The user stories already decided seats should ride on the
  membership (Open Question 2) and that the SET needs a read-only evaluator
  role (Open Question 1); neither is in the UI.
- Invitations have no expiry, no revocation, no re-issue, and no delivery.
  The identity plan (§5) specifies all four; none is built.
- `/join/{code}` confirms with an unstyled page that names nothing. The
  identity plan's step 4 ("sees the exercise name and invitation status") is
  the right target.

### 3.2 "Who is here and are we ready?" (presence and readiness)

There is no presence anywhere. The instructor learns an aircraft exists when
its plugin posts a position; the MRO learns someone is on the net when they
key up; nobody knows whether the MO's DF head is powered, whether the crew
loaded the `.fms`, or whether a tablet has microphone permission, until it
fails during the sortie. Session 1 of the rehearsal exists precisely to
discover these things by hand. The data to show them already flows through
the console: WebSocket sessions now carry a seat (D1), the radio holds
`SESSIONS`, the plugin reports `panel_seen`, head type, power and tuned
frequency with every position, and X-Plane exposes FMS entry count to the
plugin.

### 3.3 "What is each aircraft doing right now?" (live dashboards)

- **Instructor.** Aircraft are a monospace line each. There is no plugin
  link health (last report age), no "what the crew's needle shows", no
  timeline. Effects are checkboxes with no record of *when* they were
  toggled, so the debrief cannot show that the multipath bearing came 40 s
  after multipath was injected. Scenario authoring is a collapsed "+". The
  bearing log duplicates base's.
- **Mission base.** One page serves IC, AOBD and MRO with no seat
  distinction. Ops-normal clocks (user story 5.2) do not exist; the MRO
  computes "overdue" by hand. The planner's push creates a *scenario* with
  an offset beacon (good for solo practice), not an assignment on the running
  sortie, so the base map never shows the assigned areas (stories 3.1 and
  4.1). The cut plot exists (pairwise intersections, confidence polygon,
  three newest LOBs per aircraft) but there is no way to mark a cut rejected,
  which is the measure the SET wants.
- **Radio.** Visually the richest page, and an island: it does not know the
  sortie, the roster, or the ops-normal cadence, and the Form 110 lives only
  here.
- **Planner.** A different product ("CAP 487") with its own theme, font and
  disclaimer modal, reached by leaving the console. Its pattern geometry is
  now a tested pure module shared with the G1000, so it no longer has to be a
  separate page to be correct.
- **No wall display.** A mission base has a projector; nothing here is
  designed to be read from across a room.

### 3.4 "What happened, and how did we do?" (debrief)

The debrief answers "did they close" per aircraft in a paragraph. It does not
show bearing error over time, when each effect was injected, time to first
good cut, ops-normal timing, rejected cuts, or any of the six measures in
`USE_CASE_SAREX_REHEARSAL.md` as a chart, and it cannot put session 1 beside
session 3. There is no export, no SET scoring sheet, no way to bookmark a
teaching moment during the sortie and jump to it later, and no read-only link
to hand a crew.

### 3.5 Consistency and form factors

- Three visual systems: light Leaflet consoles, a dark radio page, a dark
  planner with different branding. Three headers, two clocks, two map stacks.
- The consoles have no responsive rules at all (`style.css` has only a print
  query), so a base tablet gets the desktop sidebar beside a cramped map.
  The radio page is responsive and dark; the consoles should follow it.
- The "EXERCISE TRAFFIC" marking is on the radio page only.

## 4. Proposal

Organise the product around the exercise lifecycle, which is also how the
rehearsal narrative is written: **Plan → Invite → Ready room → Live →
Debrief → Archive**. Each stage is one screen per seat, in one shell.

### 4.1 One shell

One header across console, radio and planner: exercise name and date, Zulu
clock, "EXERCISE" marking, the viewer's identity chip (seat · callsign ·
aircraft), connection state (API, net, plugin where relevant), and the
stage tabs the seat is allowed. One set of design tokens (type, colour,
spacing, status colours, one colour per aircraft used everywhere: marker,
track, card, chart series). Dark by default for the sim room with a light
option, the radio page's existing palette as the base. Responsive by design:
sidebar on desktop, bottom sheet on tablet, single column on phone. Touch
targets 44 px. Print styles for badges and ICS forms.

The planner joins the shell as a stage ("Plan") rather than a separate app:
same header and tokens, the SAR generator as a panel beside the map instead
of a modal, the disclaimer shown once per device.

### 4.2 Invite

- **Seats, not slots.** The roster is a list of seats from the user stories:
  Director; IC, AOBD, MRO; SET (evaluator, read-only); per aircraft Pilot,
  Observer, Scanner; UDF teams. A seat carries callsign, aircraft or team,
  target page and expiry. Role stays the permission class underneath.
- **Short codes.** A seat code is six characters from an unambiguous
  alphabet (`R7K4-MO`-style, Crockford base32 plus a suffix), rate-limited
  on entry, paired with a long random link token for the QR. Typeable on a
  phone in ten seconds. The 32-hex code becomes the link token only.
- **Delivery.** Email and SMS from the setup page (the identity plan's SMTP
  relay), a printable badge sheet (one card per seat with the QR, code, seat,
  callsign and the two things to bring: headset and X-Plane station), and a
  per-seat copy-link as today. Each invitation shows pending / accepted /
  joined / revoked, and has revoke and re-issue.
- **Boarding pass.** `/join/{token}` becomes a styled page that names the
  exercise, date, seat, callsign and aircraft, says what the seat needs, and
  has one Accept button. After accepting, the participant lands on their
  station's Ready room entry, not on a generic map.

### 4.3 Ready room

A lobby per exercise, visible to every seat before the sortie starts and
again between sorties.

- **Presence board.** Every seat as a tile: invited → joined → on net →
  station ready → flying. Sources: the session on the WebSocket, the radio's
  session table, the plugin's position reports and `panel_seen`.
- **Readiness checks** that turn a tile green: headset test done, mission
  net joined, microphone permission granted; for aircraft stations, plugin
  heartbeat in the last 10 s, DF head powered and tuned to the briefed
  frequency, `.fms` loaded (FMS entry count from the plugin), ATIS/briefing
  acknowledged. The director's view is a matrix; "Start sortie" warns on any
  red tile.
- **Briefing pack** per seat: the ICS 204 for the sortie, the assigned
  pattern (with its `.fms` download), the channel plan, the weather brief
  from the planner, and the exercise rules. The director can push a text
  briefing and a start countdown.
- **Lobby chat** on the existing messages table, so the base can say "CAP
  423 your mic is hot" without the net.

### 4.4 Live

- **Director console.** A timeline strip (elapsed, with markers for every
  effect injection, bearing, message, ops-normal call, retask and reveal);
  aircraft cards with link health (last report age), what the crew's needle
  shows (a small DF dial per aircraft), truth bearing and distance, a signal
  sparkline, and the head's power/frequency/squelch state; effects as a
  palette with "inject now" producing an event, plus **scripted injects**
  ("cone of confusion when within 3 NM", "multipath at T+25 min") so the
  director can watch instead of click; a presence strip; a "bookmark" button
  that drops a teaching-moment marker into the record for the debrief.
- **Mission base.** One page with three modes selected by seat and
  switchable: IC (assignments and retasking), AOBD (planner panel, cut
  plot with per-cut reject, confidence area), MRO (ops-normal clocks per
  aircraft with 30-minute overdue alarms, Form 110, net activity). An
  **assignment** object replaces today's planner-creates-a-scenario push: a
  pattern built in the planner attaches to the running sortie for one
  aircraft, appears on every base and crew map as an area with a status
  (assigned, acknowledged, active, complete), and retasking is "assign a new
  pattern", which reaches the crew's station page and, optionally, their
  plugin as an `.fms`.
- **Aircrew station page.** Own assignment card and `.fms`, channel plan,
  briefing, link health of own station, and after reveal, own debrief.
- **Ground team page.** Assignment, phone radio, bearing submission, check-in.
- **Wall board.** A read-only, truth-free display for the base projector:
  big map with aircraft, assignments and cuts; ops-normal board; who is
  transmitting; elapsed time. Reads the same filtered state as base.

### 4.5 Debrief

- **Scoring dashboard** per sortie: bearing error over time per aircraft with
  effect-injection bands behind it; CPA and time to first good cut; track
  efficiency against the assigned pattern (now computable, because the
  assignment is stored on the sortie); ops-normal timing table; cuts logged,
  rejected, and their error; a six-measure summary card per aircraft.
- **Replay** synchronised with the event list and the timeline markers;
  click a bookmark or an effect marker to jump there; the crew's needle
  reconstructed from the recorded `df`.
- **Cross-session view**: the same scenario's sorties side by side (sessions
  1–3), each measure as a small-multiple, so the SET can see whether the
  rehearsal worked.
- **SET sheet**: a checklist of the SQTR tasks demonstrated, filled from the
  measures and signed; the evaluator role is read-only (user stories, Open
  Question 1).
- **Exports**: PDF debrief report, GeoJSON/KML of tracks, bearings, cuts,
  assignments and truth, CSV of events; a read-only link to hand a crew after
  reveal.

### 4.6 Architecture that makes the above cheap

- **One event stream.** A `events(sortie_id, t, type, actor_seat, payload)`
  table and a typed WebSocket delta ("event") replace the ad hoc state
  pushes for everything that is not position: effect toggles, bearings,
  messages, ops-normal calls, assignments, retasks, bookmarks, reveal,
  presence changes. The timeline, the debrief, the wall board and the
  CalTopo relay all read it. Positions stay in `positions`.
- **Presence** in the hub keyed by session: seat, page, last seen, net
  state, plus a station heartbeat from the plugin.
- **Assignments** as first-class rows (sortie, aircraft, pattern JSON,
  status, times), written by the planner panel through the base's own
  identity (closes L-15's privilege problem by construction).
- **Serializer discipline.** `public_state()` from D1 already redacts truth
  per viewer; every new surface (wall board, station pages, CalTopo relay)
  reads through it, and the truth-boundary test walks them.

Everything in phases 1–3 is front-end and small-table work suited to Sonnet
with this document as the spec; the design-token set and the seat model are
the two things to settle first, in one short decision note, so the phases
do not each invent their own.

## 5. Things deliberately not proposed

- Replacing Leaflet or the static-file architecture. They are adequate and
  the offline tile cache is already built.
- A framework rewrite. The consoles are small enough that a shared shell,
  tokens and a few web components get the consistency without a build
  pipeline the lab does not have.
- Moving planning out of the system into CalTopo wholesale. CalTopo is the
  base staff's real-world tool and should get the assignments and tracks,
  but the truth, the scoring and the readiness model stay here.
