CAP 487

Building a Cessna 182T that barely existed.

CAP flies 182Ts. X-Plane 12 ships a Cessna 172SP with the G1000 and no 182T at all. So CAP 487, the project's own callsign for it, was built.

THE RECIPE

Cockpit from one source, skin from another, numbers from the book.

The cockpit and systems come from the simulator's own C172SP G1000, read from the operator's licensed copy at build time and never redistributed. The exterior is a pinned, GPL-licensed FlightGear C182 converted to X-Plane format. A build script supplies what makes it a 182T: the IO-540 at 230 horsepower, a three-blade constant-speed propeller and a prop lever in the 3D cockpit, 87 gallons usable, the 182T's speeds, weights and limits, with dimensions from the handbook and the type certificate. Every value in the aircraft file cites where it came from, and a test pins it there.

THE G1000 ADDITIONS

A manifold-pressure gauge and a search page.

The stock G1000 showed a 172's engine page: a tachometer and no manifold pressure, because the 172 has a fixed-pitch prop. The 182's constant-speed prop needs manifold pressure (MP) and RPM, so the build selects them and the gauge appears.

The stock G1000 also had no idea what a search pattern was. An independent extension adds search and rescue pages to the G1000's multifunction display (MFD): parallel track, creeping line, expanding square and grid, named waypoints, range rings, a DF bearing overlay, export to the flight plan, and a guarded ACTIVATE that loads the pattern into the flight management system (FMS) only when pressed twice and only when no AI pilot has control.

MEASURED, NOT ASSUMED

The performance table was flown.

The performance table in CAP 487's profile was flown, not copied: 35 throttle-and-climb points held by the aircraft's own autopilot, checked against the handbook's cruise figures (214 hp at full throttle at the test altitude against a rated 230). Handling cards and flight-test cards record the rest.

Unofficial training tool. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Civil Air Patrol.

CAP-VSAR is an unofficial training project built by Civil Air Patrol members. It is not an official Civil Air Patrol product and is not endorsed by Civil Air Patrol. "Civil Air Patrol" and "CAP" identify the organization this training is for; no CAP seal, emblem or wing patch is used here. Everything on this site is simulation: it is not for real flight planning, real direction finding or a real search.

Product names are the property of their owners and are used to identify the equipment being simulated.

X-Plane and Laminar Research, Garmin and G1000, Cessna and Skylane, and FlightGear are the property of their owners, named only to identify what the build uses or imitates. The display pictures on this page are screenshots from the simulator (its G1000 rendering with the project's own search and rescue pages) shown inside a generic drawn frame that is not a Garmin product; CAP-VSAR is unofficial and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Garmin or Laminar Research.
