## 2. Public-release risks and the line to hold

* **CAP name and marks.** Use "Civil Air Patrol" and "CAP" descriptively only. No CAP seal, propeller-triangle
  emblem, wing patch or uniform insignia anywhere (logo brief L1 forbids look-alikes; illustration I4 shows plain
  polos with no insignia). Every page footer: the disclaimer in STYLE.md ("unofficial … not endorsed by Civil Air
  Patrol"). The site title is "CAP-VSAR", never "Civil Air Patrol V-SAR" as a brand.
* **Third-party material in screenshots.** Follow commit `60f5203` (2026-10-12), which removed the Laminar C172
  render from the old site and handbook and left those sections text-only: no X-Plane cockpit or stock G1000
  captures, no Air Manager live captures (use the generated composite), no manufacturer logos as images, map
  captures on FAA sectional tiles only with the Leaflet attribution visible. Per-image status is in ASSETS.md §C.
  T16 (2026-10-18): the Airplane page now shows four G1000 MFD screenshots (the project's search and rescue pages
  on the simulator's display, in a generic drawn bezel). **G1000 display screenshots: [licence] owner decision
  pending (owner not concerned for now).** The footer's trademark line on that page names the simulator and Garmin.
  `tests/test_release_boundary.py` must be extended to cover `website-next/` the way it covers `website/`.
* **Trade names on our faceplates.** Motorola, Icom, Becker, RHOTHETA, Technisonic and Garmin names appear on
  replicas drawn from their manuals. Keep captions factual ("a training replica"), claim no affiliation, and add a
  one-line trademark acknowledgement to the footer: "Product names are the property of their owners and are used
  to identify the equipment being simulated."
* **People.** No faces, no member names, no real email addresses. The owner's name and credentials appear only
  with their consent (`[VERIFY]` in COPY.md). Sample roster addresses in captures must be `example.org`.
* **Security.** Nothing that reveals live endpoints: no LAN addresses or hostnames (the README and the old
  `website/deployment.html` carry `192.0.2.151` and `ms01`; none of that is copied), no WireGuard peer details,
  no access codes, badge codes or QR cards (the printable-badges capture shows live-format codes; do not publish),
  no Gitea address, no deploy keys. The ForeFlight page describes "the squadron's private link" without naming
  the router, ports or peers.
* **Over-claiming.** Three statements are opinion or aspiration and are written as such: the training-benefit
  argument (attributed to the program), ForeFlight (held behind `[VERIFY]`), CalTopo (roadmap). The AI Pilot page
  keeps the "proven / flown / not yet" table and the sentence that no real airplane is involved.
* **Licensing of what the site links to.** The source link points at the sanitized public copy only; the built
  aircraft is never offered for download; the exercise guide PDF is fine to publish once its screenshots pass
  ASSETS.md §C.
